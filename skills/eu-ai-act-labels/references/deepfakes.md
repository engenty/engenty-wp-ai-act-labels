# Deepfakes: the test, standard editing, exceptions

## Definition (Art. 3(60))

AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that **resembles existing persons, objects,
places, entities or events** and **would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful**.

Text is never a deepfake (it has its own rule, see text-and-chatbots.md).

## The test (Commission Guidelines, July 2026)

1. **AI generated or manipulated it.** Not a camera capture with ordinary processing.
2. **Resemblance to reality.** The subject exists, can plausibly exist or could have plausibly existed –
   an invented but photorealistic person counts; a real product, building, landscape, event counts.
3. **Could falsely appear authentic** to the *actual, foreseeable audience* of that content – not a
   hypothetical average person. Lower threshold for children and other vulnerable groups.
4. **Intent is irrelevant.** No wish to deceive is needed; good faith does not remove the duty.
5. **Context counts.** If the audience clearly does not expect authenticity (an obvious fantasy scene, a
   cartoon, an evidently stylised illustration), it does not falsely appear authentic. The "obvious"
   exception is read restrictively.

## Standard editing – no label

Edits that keep the substance, tone and purpose of the original: cropping, rotation, exposure, colour and
white balance, contrast, sharpening, noise reduction, dust and scratch removal, red-eye, lens correction,
upscaling, format conversion, small retouching (a blemish). Edits to light, colour or sound normally do not
make content a deepfake. Assess on the facts; the name of a tool decides nothing.

## Beyond standard editing – label when it looks real

Generative fill, background replacement, removing or adding people or objects, changing facial
expressions, face or body swaps, outpainting that invents scenery, voice cloning, lip-sync to new words,
changing what a sign, document or screen says. These alter meaning or context.

## Exceptions and softer regimes

- **Evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous works**: disclose appropriately
  without hampering the display or enjoyment – credits, caption, description, an unobtrusive corner mark.
- **Generated before 2 August 2026**: no retroactive duty (date of generation counts).
- **Personal, non-professional use**: outside the Act.
- **Standard film and photo production** – background plates, special effects, technical pre- and
  post-processing – is unlikely to make content falsely appear authentic.

## Examples for websites

Required:
- AI portrait of "our team", a "customer", a testimonial person, a "doctor" or "expert".
- Real photo of the shop with AI-added customers or products.
- AI image of the real product that looks like a photo of it (and of features it does not have).
- AI "photo" of a real place or event (the town square, the opening, a trade fair).
- AI video or voice of a real person, also of the owner, saying words they did not record.

Voluntary (label recommended, not required):
- Illustrations, comic or 3D-render style that nobody takes for a photo.
- Abstract backgrounds, patterns, textures, icons, decorative headers.
- Clearly fantastical scenes (a castle on a cloud).

No label:
- Camera photos, stock photos without AI, scans.
- Photos only colour-corrected, denoised, sharpened, cropped or upscaled with AI tools.
