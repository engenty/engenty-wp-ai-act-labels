# Article 50 AI Act – transparency obligations

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Chapter IV. Applies from **2 August 2026**; not postponed by the Digital
Omnibus on AI (political agreement 7 May 2026, Parliament 16 June, Council 29 June, in force 27 July 2026),
which moved the high-risk deadlines, not Article 50.

## The paragraphs

| Paragraph | Who | Duty |
|-----------|-----|------|
| 50(1) | Provider of an AI system that interacts directly with people (chatbots, voice agents, avatars) | Design it so people are informed they interact with an AI, unless obvious to a reasonably well-informed person. From the start of the first interaction. |
| 50(2) | Provider of a system that generates synthetic audio, image, video or text | Mark outputs in a machine-readable format, detectable as AI-generated or manipulated (metadata, watermarks). Not for assistive functions for standard editing or when the input is not substantially altered. Systems on the market before 2 Aug 2026: from **2 December 2026** (Omnibus grace period). |
| 50(3) | Deployer of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation | Inform the people exposed. |
| 50(4) first part | **Deployer** of a system generating or manipulating image, audio or video that is a **deepfake** | Disclose that the content was artificially generated or manipulated. Evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional works: disclose in a way that does not hamper display or enjoyment. |
| 50(4) second part | **Deployer** of a system generating or manipulating **text published to inform the public on matters of public interest** | Disclose – unless the text underwent human review or editorial control and a person holds editorial responsibility. |
| 50(5) | Providers and deployers | The information is given clearly and distinguishably, at the latest at the first interaction or exposure, meeting accessibility requirements. |

## Actors

- **Provider**: develops the AI system or places it on the market (OpenAI, Google, Adobe, Midjourney …).
- **Deployer**: uses an AI system under its authority in a professional context – a company, a freelancer,
  an agency, a shop, a club with regular economic activity. A business website owner who publishes AI
  images is a deployer.
- **Personal, non-professional use** is excluded. Earning from it regularly or doing it as part of a trade
  makes it professional.
- Outside the EU: the duties apply when the output is used in the EU.

## Timeline

- 1 Aug 2024: AI Act in force.
- 2 Feb 2025: prohibited practices; AI literacy (Art. 4).
- 2 Aug 2025: general-purpose AI model rules.
- 10 Jun 2026: final Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content, with the EU icons.
- 20 Jul 2026: Commission Guidelines on Article 50 (final; a draft came on 8 May 2026).
- 27 Jul 2026: Digital Omnibus on AI in force.
- **2 Aug 2026: Article 50 applies; enforcement powers start.**
- **2 Dec 2026: Art. 50(2) marking for systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026.**
- 2 Feb 2027: interoperability of marking solutions (Code of Practice).
- Content generated before 2 Aug 2026 needs no retroactive label; labelling it is encouraged.

## Enforcement and fines

- National market surveillance authorities, designated by each Member State (check the national
  authority before naming one). The AI Office only for systems built on general-purpose models by the
  same provider and for very large platforms.
- Fines for transparency breaches: up to EUR 15 million or 3 % of total worldwide annual turnover of the
  preceding year, whichever is higher; proportionality for SMEs and small mid-caps.

## Code of Practice

Voluntary (signed by providers and deployers). Section 1: providers' marking (at least two machine-readable
layers, e.g. signed metadata plus imperceptible watermark; C2PA is the technology that fits). Section 2:
deployers' labelling (design, placement, the EU icons in Annex I). Signing does not by itself prove
compliance; non-signatories can use the icons without signalling adherence.
