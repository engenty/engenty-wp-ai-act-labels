# AI text and chatbots

## Text on matters of public interest (Art. 50(4), second part)

Label AI-generated or AI-manipulated text when all three apply:
1. it is **published** (accessible to the public),
2. its purpose is to **inform the public**,
3. on a **matter of public interest** – politics, public health, public security, consumer safety,
   the economy, the environment, events that affect a community.

No label is needed when the text underwent **human review or editorial control** and a natural or legal
person holds **editorial responsibility** for its publication:
- human review: deliberate examination of the substance by someone with the knowledge and judgement to
  catch errors – not just spell-checking or formatting;
- editorial control: an editorial entity with authority to approve, change or reject, including fact-checking.

Most company websites: product texts, service pages, blog posts about the trade, reviewed by the owner →
no duty. Unreviewed AI news summaries, automated public-health or election information → label next to
the text ("Dieser Text wurde mit KI erstellt.").

## Chatbots and voice assistants (Art. 50(1))

- The **provider** of the system must design it so that people know they talk to an AI, from the start of
  the first interaction, unless obvious.
- A website owner embedding a chat widget should check that the widget does this (first message, header
  "KI-Assistent", avatar label) and add it in the widget's settings where it does not.
- AI avatars and cloned voices on the site that imitate real people are also deepfakes for the deployer.

## AI literacy (Art. 4)

Since 2 February 2025 providers and deployers take measures so that staff working with AI have sufficient
AI literacy. The Digital Omnibus proposal (November 2025) wanted to turn this into a task of the Commission
and the Member States; check the adopted text before telling an owner the duty is gone. Training staff who
create AI media is good practice either way.
